How Much AI Is in This Track?

Quantifying the proportion of AI-generated stems in hybrid music mixtures

Fernando Garcia de la Cruz1, David López-Ayala1, Pablo Zinemanas2, Emilio Molina2, Martín Rocamora1

1 Music Technology Group, Universitat Pompeu Fabra, Barcelona   2 BMAT Licensing S.L., Barcelona

Not only “is it AI?” but “how much AI?”

AI-generated music is increasingly used at the stem level, with producers integrating synthetic drums, basslines or vocals alongside human-performed instruments. Current AI-music detectors are binary: they treat a track as either fully AI or fully human.

We reformulate detection as regression on a continuous AI energy ratio α ∈ [0, 1], the share of a mix’s energy that comes from AI-generated stems. To study it, we build hybrid mixtures with known α from a multi-track dataset and a neural audio codec. A binary detector with over 99% accuracy on pure tracks becomes a noisy, miscalibrated estimator of α on mixed content, and its sensitivity depends on the instrument. A model with the same backbone trained directly on α reaches MAE = 0.076 and R² = 0.85 on held-out mixtures from the same pipeline.

Binary detectors assume a track is all AI or all human

State-of-the-art detectors reach near-perfect accuracy on complete tracks by spotting the spectral artifacts that neural audio decoders leave behind. But stem generators such as Diff-A-Riff and LiveBand let producers place an AI part next to human recordings, and a single label cannot describe that track.

From “is it AI?” to “how much AI?”

Counting AI stems is not enough: a loud AI lead vocal and a quiet AI rhythm guitar are both “one AI stem out of four”, yet they contribute very different amounts to what you hear. We weight each stem by its energy. Try switching stems between human and AI below.

A mix is the sum of n stems, each human-performed (ℋ) or AI-generated (𝒜). α = 0 is a fully human mix and α = 1 a fully AI one. The model predicts α̂ directly from the mixed audio, so it can report “about X% AI”.

ARIA: hybrid mixtures with known α

Studying α needs mixtures where we know exactly which stems are AI. ARIA (Authentic vs. Reconstructed Isolated Audio) encodes and decodes real stems with a neural audio codec. The reconstruction carries the codec’s artifacts but keeps the musical content, so the only difference between a real stem and its AI version is the artifact signature. For each track, ARIA then builds every real/AI combination of its stems.

Same backbone, different head

Both models share the convolutional detector of Afchar et al. (ICASSP 2025). Only the output layer and the loss change: the binary detector is trained on FMA-Medium and its EnCodec reconstructions with labels {0, 1}, and the regression model is trained on ARIA mixtures to predict α. Window scores are averaged across each mixture.

Training on α recovers the proportion

Fed hybrid mixtures it never saw in training, the binary detector still scores higher as α grows, so the codec artifacts carry proportional information. But its median score stays near 0.05 until about half of the energy is AI, with a wide spread. Used as an estimate of α, it is worse than always predicting the average (R² = −0.85). Why 5-second windows?

Window length pushes the two objectives in opposite directions. The regression model improves with longer windows, since a continuous quantity is estimated more stably over more audio. The binary detector gets worse, since averaging blurs the weak proportional signal it captures. The regression model is more accurate at both lengths, and 5 seconds is the matched comparison.

Takeaways

AI and human content are mixed

Producers combine AI-generated stems with human recordings.

Binary detection is not enough on its own

It works well on pure tracks but can’t tell how much AI is in a mix.

Estimating how much AI is a promising first step

Training on α recovers it: MAE 0.076, R² 0.85.

Limitations and next steps

Current scopeNext step
One codec at one bitrate: EnCodec, 3 kbpsOther codecs such as DAC, and commercial generators like Suno and Udio
AI stems are reconstructions of real recordings, not generated contentPaired real/AI stems from stem-conditioned generators or platform catalogs
